## 🌱 About

Matcha Studios is a small, two-person matcha business serving the GTA, with a base in Toronto and Durham. They hand-whisk every cup to order using ceremonial grade matcha, and they run pop-ups and private catering rather than taking online orders.

I'm working with them on a contract basis to build a website that reflects their brand and supports how their business actually runs. My role covers **frontend development, UI/UX design, and direct client collaboration** throughout the process.

The site is built around a few simple goals:

- Show off the drinks with a clear, appetising menu.
- Tell the story of the business and the people behind it.
- Make it easy for people to **book pop-ups and catering**.
- Stay true to the business model: no online ordering, just in-person matcha and events.

## 💚 Highlights

### 🎨 Design
- **Brand-led colour system:** matcha greens, cream and dark forest tones defined once as CSS variables and reused across every section.
- **Display + body type pairing:** a bold display font for headings and a clean body font for readability.
- **Rounded, soft UI:** pill-shaped buttons and tabs, rounded cards and an arched hero image for a friendly feel.
- **Micro-interactions:** hover lift on menu cards, image zoom on hover and press feedback on buttons.

### 🧩 Features
- **Tabbed menu:** switch between catagories of drinks, built with React state, with tags like `popular`, `bestseller` and `new` on each card.
- **Pop-ups & catering section:** explains what the business offers and gives clients a one-click enquiry link.
- **Sticky navigation:** a fixed top bar with anchor links that smooth scroll to each section.
- **Info strip:** location, opening hours and allergen info (nut-free ingredients) visible right under the hero.
- **Data-driven content:** menu items and catering options live in simple arrays, so the client's copy is easy to update.

### 🧠 Product decisions
- **No online ordering, by design.** An early version included a customisation modal and cart. After talking with the client, I removed it entirely to match how the business really operates, and replaced it with a catering and pop-up section.
